James Dolezal's new substitute teacher at Cairn University

Dr. James Dolezal was caught in a car accident, and asked a friend to teach a class for him while he was in the hospital. He was crossing the street in June and was hit by a car. He was hospitalized soon after. Because of his condition, he could not teach his Triune God class in the School of Bible and Theology at Cairn University.

Rev. Dr. Pastor Joshua Brownfield, a friend of Dolezal, was asked to take up his responsibilities and become an adjunct intructor for his class. Brownfield agreed and is teaching there now. His plan is to teach the class for the semester or until Dolezal's condition improves enough that he can reenter his position as a professor at Cairn. Brownfield teaches on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

The student population was suprised that it was not Dolezal teaching the class, but Brownfield instead. Some of the students found him to be quite likeable. One of the students, who wished to remain anonymous for this interview, said:

I found him to be quite animated, and that helped me to remain focued on the class at hand, allowing for a more productive learning enviroment. I also think he's increidibly funny with his jokes and subtle popular culture references.
However, some of the students found him to be quite different and more unlikeable than Dolezal. This is mostly because he was different and people don't really like things changing on thier watch. But some students complained about new policies. In an interview, one of the students said:
I found his "handwritten-notes-only policy" extremely annoying, because I've gotten used to taking electronic notes and finding that more efficient and easier on the hands. I also think that his jokes are fairly cringy, as most of them are Generation X and we're Generation Z, which doesn't allow for relateability.
Whether the students like him or not, Dr. Brownfield is going to teach until Dr. Dolezal can recover from his condition or someone else takes on the task if he can't come by this winter.

-Luke Brownfield
